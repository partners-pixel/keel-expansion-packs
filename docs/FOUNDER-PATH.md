# Founder path: from many ideas to a first income

Written Oct 18, 2026 for the owner of this repository. Private: keep it out of any public version of this repository.

You do not need to finish Keel, or any pack, to start. This page is a plan you can run **this week, by hand, with Claude and free tools**. The packs come later and automate what this path proves works.

---

## 1. What you told us

- You have four years as a .NET developer, and you are building Keel, an AI engineering system.
- You want a personal brand, services to offer, perhaps products or a SaaS later, and an engine that does the work for you.
- You are not sure which idea will pay, and each one seems to need two or three months before you know.
- Finding clients is hard. You would like marketing and follow-ups to run by themselves.
- You want to get better at English and at explaining things, with a personal learning plan and a memory that grows (Obsidian, a graph).
- You have seen people earn from AI videos, digital products and YouTube, but you do not know how they do it.

## 2. Three honest points first

1. **No engine can tell you what people will pay for. Only people paying can.** Building the full engine first, to find out which business to start, is the slowest route. It also breaks Keel's own Principle 15: *a feature enters the engine only when a real project shows the need.* Apply that rule to your business too. Do the work by hand for a few weeks, write down what repeats, then automate the repeats.
2. **Clients buy from a person they trust.** AI can do most of the preparation: research, drafts, reminders, proposals, turning a call into a requirement. You send the messages and you take the calls. LinkedIn's user agreement forbids bots and automated messaging, and losing your account would cost the one channel you need most.
3. **"AI cartoon videos for money" is a crowded, shrinking bet.** Since July 2025 YouTube refuses to pay for mass-produced or templated content ("inauthentic content"), and Meta, X and TikTok have similar rules. Children's content has extra legal rules and limited ads. That path suits someone with a large original catalogue, not a first experiment.

## 3. Your advantage

Most people posting about AI have never built anything serious with it. You are a working .NET developer building a system that makes AI agents trustworthy: merge gates, evidence tied to commits, lessons that turn into checks. Businesses on the Microsoft stack want AI features and automation, and they worry about exactly what Keel is about: mistakes, data leaks and costs running away.

So your position can be one line:

> I add AI features and automations to .NET business software, built with checks, so they are safe to use.

Your content then comes from your real work: building Keel, and solving .NET and AI problems. You never have to invent topics.

## 5. The plan in two-week experiments

Instead of one two-to-three-month bet, run short experiments. Each one has a goal, an action, a measure and a rule for what to do next. The hours are an example; set your own.

| Weeks | Goal | What you do | Measure | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Be findable | Rewrite your LinkedIn headline and About section around the one-line position. Post 3 times a week: 2 text posts and 1 carousel | Profile views, comments, messages received | Keep going regardless; this is the base |
| 3–4 | Start conversations | 5 personal messages a day to people with a real reason to hear from you: former colleagues and managers, companies you know use .NET, people who commented on your posts. Claude drafts, you edit and send | Replies, calls booked | 3 or more calls: keep the offer. No calls after about 50 messages: change the offer's wording or the audience, not the channel |
| 5–6 | First pilot | Offer the fixed-scope pilot to the warmest conversation; write the scope on one page | A signed scope; a delivered pilot | One pilot done: write the case study. None: test offer B or C for two weeks |
| 7–8 | Proof | Publish the case study (with permission, client details changed); keep posting | Inbound messages | Repeat weeks 3 to 6 with the case study in hand |
| 9–12 | Repeat | Second and third pilots; note what every client asked for | Paid work; repeated requests | Three clients asking for the same thing is a product idea (a template, a starter kit, later a SaaS) |

## 6. What Claude does for you now, without Keel

| Task | How | You keep |

| Posts | Keep a notes file of what you did and learned each day. Claude turns one note into a draft in your voice | The edit and the press of "Post". Never post what you do not understand |
| Carousels | Claude writes the slides as HTML; export to PDF and upload as a LinkedIn document | Approving the look |
| Prospect research | Claude reads a company's public website and suggests one real reason to contact them | Deciding who to contact |
| Messages and follow-ups | Claude drafts. A simple sheet tracks name, company, why, date sent, follow-up date and status | Sending, and every reply to a person |
| Calls | Claude turns your call notes or transcript into a one-page requirement brief, then a proposal draft | The price and the promise |
| Delivery | Claude Code builds the pilot. Later, Keel's software pack, whose "delivery mode" for client work is built when a real delivery client exists. Your first client is that evidence | Approving scope and release |

Keep a **repeat log**: one line every time you do a task (for example, "wrote a post from a note", "researched a company", "turned a call into a brief"). A task logged three times is worth a template. Three templates in one area are worth a Keel pack. This log is the evidence that sets the pack order in PLAN.md, and the source of a future SaaS idea.

Share only what a task needs with an AI: public business information, your own notes. Never a client's private data or passwords.

## 7. English and explaining: a personal learning plan

About 20 to 25 minutes a day. Run it in a Claude Project with its own instructions and a mistakes file.

1. **Write (10 minutes).** Write about 150 words on what you did or learned today. Claude corrects it, explains your top three mistakes, and adds them to the mistakes file. This file works like Keel's lessons: the same mistake twice means you practise that rule on purpose.
2. **Speak (10 minutes).** Record a 2-minute voice note explaining one .NET or AI idea, as if to a client. Transcribe it with your phone or with Whisper, which is free. Claude scores it against a fixed rubric: clarity, structure, grammar, filler words.
3. **Review (5 minutes).** Read the mistakes file before you start the next day.

**Every week:** one 5-minute recorded explanation. **Every month:** the same test prompt as month one, scored with the same rubric. Track two numbers: mistakes per 100 words, and how often an old mistake comes back. Generated material is not learning; only the monthly score shows learning.

You can post in English with AI help while you improve: you write the idea, Claude fixes the language, you read it back until you would say it the same way. Writing comes first. Voice and video come when the monthly scores say you are ready.

## 8. Memory: keep it simple

- One private notes folder of Markdown files in a private git repository, opened with Obsidian: `ideas/`, `repeat-log.md`, `learning/` (the mistakes file and monthly scores), `posts/`, `clients/` (notes only, never secrets or private data).
- Obsidian shows the links between notes as a graph. Claude Code can read the folder in a session. No graph database or special engine is needed now.
- Later, the skill registry in PLAN.md section 8 writes its Markdown views with the same `[[links]]`, so it can join this vault.

## 9. AI video, when you are ready (not now)

A simple, honest pipeline for a 60 to 90 second explainer:
1. **Script:** turn your best-performing LinkedIn post into a script with Claude.
2. **Voice:** your own recording. Or text-to-speech whose licence allows commercial use (for example Kokoro, Apache-2.0), with a disclosure where the platform requires one.
3. **Visuals:** a screen recording of real code, slides from the carousel, or images you have the right to use.
4. **Edit:** any simple editor, or FFmpeg, Motion Canvas or MoviePy if you want it done by code.
5. **Captions:** Whisper (free) creates them; check them yourself.
6. **Publish:** upload as private, watch it once, set YouTube's altered-or-synthetic-content disclosure if anything realistic was made by AI, then make it public.

Keel's `video` pack (PLAN.md, pack 6) automates and checks these steps later: captions in sync, loudness, rights, disclosure.
