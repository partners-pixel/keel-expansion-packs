# Founder path: one focus, one ladder

Written Oct 18, 2026 for the owner of this repository. Private: keep it out of any public version of this repository.

This is your one-page plan. You can start it this week, by hand, with Claude and free tools. You do not need to wait for Keel 1.0 or for any pack. The packs come later and automate what this path proves works.

---

## 1. Your focus, in one line

> **I build finished software quickly with AI engines that check their own work.**

Sell that as finished results. Everything else on your list either markets that, or is a product it leads to later.

## 2. The basic motive of Keel

**The person using the engine brings the knowledge of their field. The engine brings the way of working.**

Keel does not need to know everything. An accountant knows accounting, a teacher knows teaching, and you know .NET. They tell the engine what to make and why, give it their references, and make the decisions only a person should make. The engine plans, does the work in small steps, checks each step with programs, and learns from every mistake.

| Who | Brings |
|---|---|
| The person using Keel | What to make, why, the rules of their field, references, and every decision about money, law, risk and taste |
| A pack | How to make one kind of thing well, and how to check it: an app, a guide, a carousel, a video |
| The kernel | How to run any work safely: plans, approvals, evidence, records, lessons |

So Keel needs only **a few packs** (kinds of output), while the **fields** people use them in are unlimited. That is why you do not have to build a "universe" of all knowledge first.

## 3. Why this focus, looking ahead

AI is making "making things" cheap for everyone. Four things keep their value:

1. **Knowing what is worth making**: real customer problems.
2. **Trust that it is correct.**
3. **Reach**: people who know you.
4. **Owning a system that repeats.**

You already have **2 and 4**: your engines. You are missing **1 and 3**. So the next months should get you customers and reach. Client work gives you money and real problems. Writing in public gives you reach.

## 5. The ladder

Each step pays for and proves the next one.

| When | What you sell | Who pays | Proof you use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Now** | Fixed-scope software and AI features for small businesses (.NET), built with your engine | Business owners | Your build log, Keel, small demos |
| **Now, alongside** | Nothing yet: a public build log on LinkedIn and a small knowledge website | Nobody; it brings clients | Your real work |
| **Months 3–6** | "I set up an AI team that checks its own work" for developer teams | Companies with developers | Keel |
| **Months 6–12** | Products: a guide or course, packs, a SaaS that 3 clients asked for, and later Keel itself after 1.0 | The audience you built | Everything above |

Example fixed-scope packages for "Now" (fictional examples; you set the price):
- **Document Q&A** inside an existing ASP.NET app, over the company's own files, with an approval step and an audit log.
- **Inbox or ticket triage** that drafts replies a person approves before anything is sent.
- **A weekly report** from the company's SQL Server data, written as a short summary for a manager.

Start with one small pilot at a fixed price, or a free pilot in return for a case study and an honest testimonial you may publish (never an invented one).

## 8. The first 12 weeks, in two-week experiments

Each experiment has a goal, an action, a measure and a rule for what to do next.

| Weeks | Goal | What you do | Measure | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Be findable | Rewrite your LinkedIn headline and About section around the one-line focus. Post 3 times a week: 2 text posts and 1 carousel | Profile views, comments, messages received | Keep going regardless; this is the base |
| 3–4 | Start conversations | 5 personal messages a day to people with a real reason to hear from you: former colleagues and managers, companies you know use .NET, people who commented on your posts. Claude drafts, you edit and send | Replies, calls booked | 3 or more calls: keep the offer. No calls after about 50 messages: change the offer's wording or the audience, not the channel |
| 5–6 | First pilot | Offer the fixed-scope pilot to the warmest conversation; write the scope on one page | A signed scope; a delivered pilot | One pilot done: write the case study. None: test the developer-team offer for two weeks |
| 7–8 | Proof | Publish the case study (with permission, client details changed); keep posting | Inbound messages | Repeat weeks 3 to 6 with the case study in hand |
| 9–12 | Repeat | Second and third pilots; note what every client asked for | Paid work; repeated requests | Three clients asking for the same thing is a product idea |

| Task | How | You keep |
|---|---|---|
| Posts | A notes file of what you did and learned each day; Claude turns one note into a draft in your voice | The edit and the press of "Post". Never post what you do not understand |
| Carousels | Claude writes the slides as HTML; export to PDF and upload as a LinkedIn document | Approving the look |
| Prospect research | Claude reads a company's public website and suggests one real reason to contact them | Deciding who to contact |
| Messages and follow-ups | Claude drafts; a simple sheet tracks name, company, why, date sent, follow-up date and status | Sending, and every reply to a person |
| Calls | Claude turns your notes into a one-page requirement brief, then a proposal draft | The price and the promise |
| Delivery | Claude Code builds the pilot, the way your engine built a large business app before | Approving scope and release |

Keep a **repeat log**: one line every time you do a task. A task logged three times is worth a template. Three templates in one area are worth a Keel pack. This log sets the pack order in PLAN.md, and it is where a future SaaS idea comes from.

Share only what a task needs with an AI: public business information and your own notes. Never a client's private data or passwords.

## 10. English and explaining

About 20 to 25 minutes a day, in a Claude Project with its own instructions and a mistakes file.

1. **Write (10 minutes).** About 150 words on what you did or learned today. Claude corrects it, explains your top three mistakes, and adds them to the mistakes file.
2. **Speak (10 minutes).** Record a 2-minute voice note explaining one .NET or AI idea, as if to a client. Transcribe it with your phone or with Whisper, which is free. Claude scores it on a fixed rubric: clarity, structure, grammar, filler words.
3. **Review (5 minutes).** Read the mistakes file before you start the next day.

Every month, take the same test as in month one and score it with the same rubric. Track mistakes per 100 words, and how often an old mistake comes back. Only that monthly score shows learning; generated material does not.

## 12. AI video, later

When your posts show which topics people want, turn your best post into a 60 to 90 second explainer:
1. Claude writes the script.
2. You record the voice yourself, or use licensed text-to-speech.
3. Use a screen recording of real code, or your carousel slides.
4. Edit it simply.
5. Whisper creates captions; you check them.
6. Upload as private, watch it once, set YouTube's disclosure if anything realistic was made by AI, then publish.

Keel's `video` pack automates and checks these steps later.
